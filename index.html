betterSchedule — Problem Statement

Students experience a disconnect between academic planning platforms (e.g., Atlas, Canvas) and daily execution tools (e.g., Google Calendar). These systems do not integrate or adapt to individual workload patterns, forcing students to manually reconstruct their schedules across fragmented tools.

This results in scheduling fatigue, increased cognitive load, and inefficient coordination for group work. As a result, students lack clear visibility into their true time commitments and struggle to manage their workload in a way that aligns with their personal productivity style.

betterSchedule - Product Requirements Document (PRD)

Open Interactive Prototype

Product Goals

  • Centralize academic planning and daily scheduling
  • Reduce time spent organizing schedules
  • Improve collaboration for group work
  • Provide intelligent scheduling assistance

Users

Students will use this product to:

  • Import assignments and deadlines automatically
  • View their academic workload in one place
  • Block study time effectively
  • Coordinate schedules with teammates

User Needs/Wants

From interviews, students expressed desire for:

  • Automated syllabus import
  • Better schedule visibility
  • Customizable and personalized UI
  • Easier collaboration tools
  • Intelligent planning support

Assumptions

Before conducting interviews, I assumed:

  • Students struggled mainly with procrastination
  • Existing calendar tools were sufficient

However, interviews revealed that:

  • Students spend more time organizing schedules than executing tasks
  • Tool fragmentation causes planning fatigue
  • Collaboration and schedule visibility are major issues

Features

1. Auto Syllabus Sync

Automatically extracts assignments and deadlines from syllabus PDFs.

Goal: Eliminate manual entry.

Use Case: Student uploads syllabus - deadlines are automatically added to schedule.

2. Smart Scheduling Assistant

AI-assisted planning that suggests study blocks based on workload and availability.

Goal: Help students distribute work across their schedule.

3. Collaboration Scheduling

Shared availability and meeting coordination tools.

Goal: Reduce communication overhead for group projects.

4. Customizable Interface

Themes, layouts, and aesthetic personalization.

Goal: Allow students to tailor the interface to their preferences.

5. Focus Tools

Includes Pomodoro timer, motivational prompts, and mindfulness exercises.

Goal: Support productivity and reduce burnout.

Next Steps

To further develop betterSchedule:

  • Conduct usability testing on the prototype
  • Test syllabus parsing workflow
  • Validate collaboration features

Implementation Needs

To build this product, development would require:

  • Calendar API integration
  • PDF parsing system for syllabus imports
  • Collaboration backend
  • AI scheduling assistant

Future Improvements

  • Cross-platform sync across devices
  • Integration with university systems
  • Advanced workload analytics

BetBuddies — Overview

Project Overview

BetBuddies — Social Accountability Platform for Goal Retention

A product concept developed through 50+ user interviews to solve inconsistent habit formation using financial commitment and social pressure.

Focus: Behavioral design, user research, product strategy.

Key Insight

Users do not fail because they lack motivation — they fail because there is no immediate consequence or reward.

  • Accountability is the missing reinforcement.
  • Social pressure creates follow-through.
  • Immediate feedback loops drive behavior change.

Ecosystem

  • Users (goal setters).
  • Friends (accountability).
  • Payment processors (transactions).
  • App stores (distribution).
  • External influences (social pressure, productivity culture).

Key Risk: Dependence on payment platforms as gatekeepers.

Validation & Decision

  • 50 interviews conducted.
  • Majority do not use existing tools.
  • Strong demand for reminders, rewards, and tracking.

Decision: Go.

Key Learnings

  • Users do not adopt tools that add friction.
  • Behavior change requires reinforcement loops.
  • Initial assumptions (gym goals) were wrong — research matters.
  • Product direction must evolve with user data.

BetBuddies — Key Problems

The Problem

College students (18–22) consistently fail to maintain habits like sleep, despite existing tools like reminders and trackers.

  • 61% feel physically drained when goals fail.
  • 52% cite academic workload as the primary disruption.
  • Most rely on self-discipline without structured tools.

Opportunity: Current solutions are high-friction and easy to ignore.

BetBuddies — User Research

Research Process

50 interviews with University of Michigan students.

  • 25 broad goal interviews.
  • 25 focused sleep interviews.

Key Findings

  • Sleep and wellness emerged as the top goal category.
  • Users want consistency, not just improvement.
  • No dominant solution exists in the market.

Pain Points

  • Academic workload: 52%.
  • Schedule inconsistency: 25%.
  • Social distractions: 17%.
  • Phone usage: 13%.

Personas

Personas (Ethan, Jason, Cynthia) revealed shared challenges across job seekers, students, and wellness-focused users.

  • Rejection fatigue during job searches.
  • Habit inconsistency in sleep and wellness goals.
  • Lack of visible progress.

Needs: Small wins, accountability, immediate reinforcement.

BetBuddies — Solution

Solution

BetBuddies introduces a commitment-based system:

  • Users deposit money toward a goal.
  • Invite friends for accountability.
  • Track progress daily.
  • Success returns the deposit; failure loses it.

Outcome: Combines financial stakes with social pressure to sustain habits.

How It Works

  1. Set a goal (example: sleep before 12 AM).
  2. Deposit money.
  3. Invite an accountability group.
  4. Track progress.
  5. Outcome: success returns money; failure triggers a penalty.

BetBuddies — Future Build

Future Build

  • UI mockups and prototype screens.
  • System architecture and MVP roadmap.
  • Monetization and go-to-market strategy.

Synthlab 🧬

Working in clinical research taught me how hard it is to get clean, usable data — and how much harder it gets when patient privacy is on the line. SynthLab is my answer to that tension: a tool that generates statistically faithful synthetic datasets so researchers can test, validate, and iterate without ever touching real patient records.

Heartline Care Companion 💚

Inspired by the workflows and care routines I saw in the hospital, Heartline is my attempt to bring that same sense of support and connection into a mobile app. It’s designed to help users stay connected to their wellness goals through gentle reminders, scheduling, and mood check-ins — all wrapped in a UI that flexes with their energy levels.

Smart Record App 🩺

After working in health care for a couple of years, I've seen and work with different format of the EMR system. I've seen the wonderful functions and how it all connect to one another, how it connects to patient's care team, and how managable it can be. But I've also seen its flaws, its design, and I wanted to do something that represents me and my needs. Smart Record is designed to help students and new care team members understand inpatient documentation in a safe, thoughtful way. It walks through real vitals flows, highlights what’s concerning, and explains how each data point shapes clinical decisions. Because learning care shouldn’t feel intimidating

I'm prototyping guided prompts, optional mood data syncing, and a "soft data" dashboard caregivers can reference when supporting loved ones.
